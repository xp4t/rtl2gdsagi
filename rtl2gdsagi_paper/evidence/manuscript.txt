    Candidate-Bound Evidence for RTL-to-GDS
       Signoff: A Frozen-Source Evaluation

Rithwik Vallabhan TV, Sreejeesh SG, Jayaraj U Kidav, and Nanditha N Varma

                            NIELIT Calicut, India
             rithwik@calicut.nielit.in, sreejeesh@nielit.gov.in

      Abstract. RTL2GDSAGI binds native verification evidence to the im-
      plementation selected for release. With frozen controller af31b39f6782,
      seven SKY130 designs spanning 16–1024 mapped cells complete the 21-
      stage verification contract twice. All seven pairs reproduce routed DEF,
      mapped and routed netlists, extracted parasitics, and date-normalized
      GDS; the 1,024-cell shift register uses one routing worker. Six constructed
      classes of stale or incomplete evidence are blocked in three consistency ex-
      ecutions each. We also re-execute 20 recorded model proposals, generated
      under snapshot 18d948815ac6, on the frozen controller. Failed native-
      check fractions are 0/3 for spacing, 0/3 for power-network construction,
      and 14/14 for timing. These class-specific observations describe proposal
      states in iterative repair trajectories. A controlled padding ablation ex-
      plains why a narrow timing pass from the earlier snapshot fails under
      the frozen source. The artifact contains executable configurations, native
      reports, source snapshots, and scripts generating the measured tables.

      Keywords: RTL-to-GDS · physical verification · artifact provenance ·
      electronic design automation · verified repair


1   Introduction
OpenROAD and OpenLane provide open infrastructure for synthesis and phys-
ical implementation [1,2]. A release decision must combine several kinds of evi-
dence: design rule checking (DRC) evaluates geometry, layout versus schematic
comparison (LVS) evaluates connectivity, and timing evaluates a constrained
circuit with its parasitics. The controller must establish which implementation
and verification inputs produced each result. A zero-marker report can concern
a different layout or omit required rules. A repair can also invalidate previously
clean evidence.
    RTL2GDSAGI makes this relationship explicit. Native tools produce artifacts
and reports; a content manifest identifies the release candidate; and gate records
specify their consumed inputs, parser contracts, and verdicts. Model proposals
pass through typed authorization and native rechecking. This paper examines
three questions: which constructed evidence failures are blocked, which recorded
proposal states survive native checks, and which implementation artifacts repro-
duce under a frozen controller. Its contribution is the executable contract and

measured integration of these mechanisms. Native synthesis, placement, routing,
and verification algorithms remain those of the selected EDA tools.


2     Evidence Contract
2.1   Candidate identity and release checks
The controller executes characterization, lint, simulation, constraints, synthesis,
synthesis equivalence, pre-layout timing, floorplan, power-network construction,
placement, clock-tree synthesis (CTS), post-CTS timing, routing, extraction,
post-route timing, GDS stream-out, DRC, LVS, antenna checking, routed equiv-
alence, and signoff. Pre-layout timing is advisory; post-CTS timing gates hold
slack, and post-route timing gates setup and hold. Release requires all declared
hard obligations to have positive evidence.
    The artifact ledger stores each output’s producer, path, size, and SHA-256
hash. Directory hashing uses relative filenames and contents. Writing H for
SHA-256, the candidate identity is

                 C = H(T, {(k, H(Ak ))}k∈B , P, V, H(K), H(I)) ,                 (1)

where T is the top, B the bound artifacts, P the recorded process-design kit
(PDK) identity, V the tool identities, K the run configuration, and I the typed
configuration state. P includes name, root, cell library, and corner. The layer
map and verification decks are hashed members of B; the whole installed PDK
is not content-hashed.
    A gate records consumed and produced artifact hashes, report identity, parser
contract, and accepted tool identity. Release revalidation rehashes files, checks
lineage, and requires DRC and LVS to consume the selected GDS. Native KLay-
out arguments are checked against the selected input and deck bytes. The certifi-
cation identifier commits to the candidate and gate records. Figure 1 summarizes
the decision boundary.

2.2   Native obligations and repair
DRC requires a usable report, the expected top, and the approved active-rule in-
ventory under the exact deck. This evaluation uses 211 normalized categories; 213
raw declarations include two approved aliases. A zero-marker report with missing
categories fails the inventory check. LVS requires agreement among process sta-
tus, log verdict, and a native database with actual circuit cross-references, plus an
approved deck containing executable compare. Stream-out requires nonempty ge-
ometry, resolved references, and mapped layers. The power distribution network
(PDN) check requires supply nets, rails, straps, and vertical via connections.
    Extraction compares logical net identities in Standard Parasitic Exchange
Format (SPEF) and routed Design Exchange Format (DEF), rejects missing or
duplicate nets, and requires complete parasitic sections. Native static timing
analysis (STA) separately requires finite setup/hold slack and zero unannotated

   A typed change rolls execution back to its earliest affected stage and invali-
dates downstream evidence. Three attempts per stage and 24 diagnosis attempts

revisit a previously failed stage fingerprint, including its inputs and effective
configuration. Formal equivalence checks both synthesis and routed netlists us-

under [collect *] and group * preserves coupled-state proof structure. Fresh
counter and shift-register controls prove equivalent, while a retained counter
mutant with a forced flop fails equivalence.


3      Experimental Protocol

3.1     One frozen implementation

All primary implementation runs, fresh proposal executions, and repeated fault
checks use source fingerprint af31b39f6782. It hashes sorted Python source
paths and bytes. The frozen archive also retains non-Python resources. Current
HEAD has this same implementation fingerprint; later Git commits change
experiment scripts and manuscript material. The proposal-origin snapshot,
18d948815ac6, is separate: it generated the archived live trajectories. Table 1
assigns each result to its execution and proposal origin.
    The primary designs are a register, registered multiplexer, eight-bit ALU,
counter, bidirectional shift register, four-entry FIFO, and 1,024-stage serial shift
register. Each has a self-checking testbench. The PDK is SKY130A, Volare re-
vision bdc9412b3e46, HD library, typical 25 ◦ C and 1.8 V corner. The pinned
container uses OpenROAD 41a51eaf4ca2 and KLayout 0.28.2; pre-layout Open-
STA reports 3.1.0. Baseline clocks are 10.0 ns, with 0.25 ns uncertainty and 0.05
pF output load.

   Each design runs twice from RTL through signoff. The shift register uses
one routing worker; its otherwise unchanged eight-worker pair is retained as an
ablation. It uses two padding sites, 25% core utilization, 0.35 placement density,
an 80% antenna prediction margin, eight repair iterations, and passive fillers.
Final antenna checks use the original limits. All typed parameters, commands,
tool/PDK identities, and candidate hashes are retained. Cell area comes from
synthesis-library reports, die area from routed DEF, and timing from extracted
parasitics. Concurrent-job runtime is an observation of this environment rather
than isolated tool-performance measurement.


3.2    Coverage and fixed-proposal replay

The coverage unit is one of six author-constructed fault classes: stale same-top
GDS, missing front-end-of-line (FEOL) rule categories, LVS without compare,
missing PDN vias, missing layer map, and artifact substitution after a verified
repair. Three executions per class check consistent behavior. Fixtures run native
tools where applicable and exercise production parsers, structural checks, invo-
cation checks, or gate lineage. Their selection against declared obligations makes
this coverage testing, with no population false-pass-rate estimate.
    The recorded proposals come from nine live Claude Sonnet 5 (Anthropic,
claude-sonnet-5) episodes: three each for ALU spacing, register PDN con-
struction, and ALU setup timing. Replay reconstructs complete effective config-
uration states, including characterization seeds, curated changes that preceded
model calls, and cumulative authorized model changes. Successful reconstruc-
tions match the archived final IR before execution. All 20 states are then run
independently from RTL on the frozen controller with one routing worker and
further repair disabled. No new model calls generate these states. The supple-
mentary record retains the original episodes, earlier measurements, reconstruc-
tion checks, and incomplete scale cases.
    The hypothetical no-recheck gate promotes every authorized state. We report
its failed-native-check fraction separately for each originating fixture class. The
proposal sequence was collected under native feedback, so these fractions describe

fixed states rather than the trajectory of an independently executed controller
without rechecking.

4.2   Fault coverage and native checker scope

Every constructed class is blocked in all three executions (Table 5). A na-
tive zero-marker result on stale geometry fails selected-input binding. Disabling
FEOL produces an incomplete inventory; a fabricated match lacks circuit cross-
references. Removed vias fail supply connectivity, omitted mapping fails layer
checks, and substituted post-repair GDS invalidates cached gate lineage.
    The OpenLane follow-up audits actual pinned stage/checker interfaces using
paired controls. Table 6 reports the obligations supported by those interfaces.
The previous modern-deck wrapper omitted runtime flags, leaving its rules inac-
tive; it is excluded as an active-DRC baseline. Legacy native configuration and
modern explicitly configured controls exercise the intended geometry rules. Pub-
lic pinned source matches the retained wrapper, and PDK options are recorded.
The modern adapter is explicitly identified; personal maintainer endorsement
is not claimed. Modern configured controls report metal/poly/off-grid mark-
ers 3/1/4 while paired clean inputs remain clear; the legacy deck omits the
tested poly rule. Reporting markers and enforcing a release decision are sep-
arate obligations. This study executes isolated interfaces, without estimating

4.4   Explaining the narrow timing repair
The archived repaired ALU configuration passes at +1.586 ps under its recorded
source but fails at −0.654 ps under the frozen controller, at the same 3 ns period.
We test CTS-padding reapplication, routing-padding reapplication, and routing
workers in a 2 × 2 × 2 factorial ablation. Placement padding stays at one site;
clock, uncertainty, load, RTL, and PDK remain fixed. Table 8 gives the measured
outcomes.

5   Related Work

OpenROAD and OpenLane provide implementation infrastructure [1,2]. MET-
RICS2.1 structures flow measurement and parameter tuning [4], and PROV
represents provenance relationships [5]. ChatEDA studies model-driven EDA
task planning, script generation, and execution [6]. ORFS-agent optimizes
OpenROAD-flow-scripts parameters [7]; MCP4EDA connects model interaction
to RTL-to-GDS execution and backend synthesis feedback [8]. FlowFixer-EDA
uses log extraction, patch validation, and native tool feedback for diagnosis
and repair [9]. EDAid uses multiple model-controlled agents for EDA workflow
execution [10]. Ghose et al. study executable OpenROAD changes and measured
quality of results [11].
    ChiBench supplies RTL designs for EDA-tool testing [12]. DRVerify docu-
ments physical-verification runset testing with known legal and violating struc-
tures [13]. These works motivate checking rule execution and intended cov-
erage in addition to marker counts. The present contribution combines con-
structed native/ledger faults, candidate lineage, fresh recorded-proposal checks,
and artifact-level repeat evidence. Comparable contracts in other controllers are
not excluded by this study.


6   Scope and Conclusion

The evidence establishes six-class coverage, class-specific proposal outcomes, and
exact repeat levels on one frozen implementation. All seven primary pairs repro-
duce their routed and date-normalized artifacts. The replay’s failures are con-
fined to the timing fixture, and the padding experiment explains why its earlier
marginal pass does not transfer to HEAD. The primary circuits are small and
synthetic, including a regular 1,024-cell shift register. They provide no popula-
tion repair rate or industrial-scale qualification. The local contract uses a single
typical corner and excludes multi-corner timing, on-chip variation, power in-
tegrity, and foundry qualification. Hashing also assumes trusted tools, controller,
host, and PDK. Identity and approved inventory do not prove completeness of
foundry rules or immunity to forged records.
    The supplement retains nine original live repair episodes, four larger ac-
cumulator episodes and their measured outcomes, earlier observations, known-
failed-fingerprint proposals, and incomplete scale probes. These cohorts remain
separate from fresh proposal-state executions. Raw logs retain accepted up-
dates and final native artifacts; rollback can overwrite numbered intermediate
prompt/response and implementation files. The current regression record is 955
passed, 15 skipped, zero failures/errors.
