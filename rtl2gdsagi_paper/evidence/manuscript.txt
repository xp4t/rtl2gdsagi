      Abstract. A clean physical-verification report is useful only if its checks
      cover the implementation selected for release. RTL2GDSAGI binds RTL,
      constraints, layout, parasitics, verification inputs, and native gate records
      to a candidate manifest and revalidates these identities before local sig-
      noff. We evaluate 7 SKY130 designs spanning 16–1024 mapped cells and
      complete the declared 21-stage contract, including synthesis and routed
      equivalence, extracted-parasitic timing, design rule checking (DRC), lay-
      out versus schematic comparison (LVS), and antenna checks. In a retro-
      spective no-recheck replay, 11 of 20 authorized model proposals fail na-
      tive checks and 2 lack a required target check. Nine live repair episodes
      cover spacing, power-network construction, and stressed timing; 7 com-
      plete the verification contract and 2 escalate. A detection coverage study
      exercises six author-constructed stale or incomplete evidence classes; all
      six are blocked. Three repetitions per class check consistent behavior and
      are not independent experimental trials. At an unchanged 3 ns period,
      one archived-source ALU repair changes setup slack from -0.326192 ns to
      0.001586 ns; repeated source checks show this margin is fragile. Repeated
      implementation runs produce 6 identical routed-DEF pairs and 6 iden-
      tical GDS pairs after zeroing timestamp payloads; raw GDS hashes and
      candidate identifiers can differ. All tables are generated from retained
      native artifacts and audit logs.

      Keywords: RTL-to-GDS · physical verification · artifact provenance ·
      electronic design automation · verified repair


1   Introduction
OpenROAD and OpenLane provide practical open infrastructure for synthesis
and physical implementation [1,2]. Their native checks answer different ques-
tions: timing concerns a constrained circuit and parasitic network; DRC concerns
geometry under a rule deck; and LVS concerns the layout and its schematic ref-
erence. A controller must establish that these answers support the same release
candidate. Zero DRC markers can accompany a stale layout or an incomplete
rule inventory, and a success message can appear without a real LVS compar-
ison. Repairs create an additional opportunity for previously valid evidence to
become stale.
2       R. Vallabhan TV et al.

    RTL2GDSAGI is a Python controller that combines native tool execution
with content-bound gate evidence. A model proposes a configuration change; de-
terministic code authorizes the changed surface and requires the affected native
checks before promotion. This paper tests three questions: whether these checks
reject specific false-clean fixtures, whether live repairs survive native rechecking,
and which implementation artifacts reproduce across runs.
    The contributions are an executable candidate and gate contract, a native
fault study that isolates six false-clean conditions, and a measured repair study
with a comparator that accepts proposals without rechecking. The underlying
synthesis, placement, routing, and verification algorithms come from the selected
EDA tools. We claim detection of the tested fault classes; we do not claim
that provenance alone establishes manufacturing qualification or that other con-
trollers cannot implement comparable contracts.


2     Candidate and Gate Contract

2.1   Execution and content identity

The 21 stages are characterization, lint, simulation, constraint generation, syn-
thesis, synthesis equivalence, pre-layout timing, floorplan, power-network gen-
eration, placement, clock-tree synthesis, post-CTS timing, routing, extraction,
post-route timing, GDS stream-out, DRC, LVS, antenna, routed equivalence,
and signoff. Pre-layout timing is advisory; post-CTS timing gates hold slack,
and post-route timing gates both setup and hold. Signoff requires every declared
hard obligation to be supported by positive evidence.
    Verilator and Icarus Verilog provide lint and self-checking simulation. Yosys
and EQY provide synthesis and equivalence [3]. OpenROAD provides physical
implementation, extraction, and physical-stage timing. KLayout streams and
verifies the layout. The artifact ledger records a producer, size, path, and SHA-
256 digest for each output. Directory identities hash relative filenames and file
contents. The candidate manifest additionally records configuration, constraints,
selected PDK identity, and tool identities. The layer map and DRC/LVS decks
are bound inputs because their contents change the meaning of the verification
results.
    Writing H for SHA-256, the candidate identity is

                 C = H(T, {(k, H(Ak ))}k∈B , P, V, H(K), H(I)) ,                 (1)

where T is the top, B the bound artifact keys, P the selected PDK identity, V
the recorded tool identities, K the run configuration, and I the typed configura-
tion state. The PDK identity includes its name, root, standard-cell library, and
corner; it is not a hash of every file in the PDK installation. Verification decks
and the stream-out map are hashed as members of B.
    Each gate declares its consumed and produced artifact keys, report key,
parser contract, and accepted tool identity. Release revalidation rehashes the

2.2   Required native evidence
DRC requires a usable report, the expected top, and the approved rule inventory
under the exact deck identity. The selected runset has 211 normalized active
categories; 213 raw declarations include two narrowly approved aliases. A zero-
marker report with missing categories is insufficient. LVS requires agreement
among exit status, log verdict, and a native database containing actual circuit
cross-references. Its deck must contain an executable comparison and belong to
the approved set. Stream-out checks nonempty geometry, resolved references,
and layer numbers compatible with the PDK mapping. The power distribution
network (PDN) check requires supply nets, follow-pin rails, straps, and vertical
via connections. These are structural obligations; IR drop and electromigration
are outside the implemented contract.
    Extraction compares logical net identities in Standard Parasitic Exchange
Format (SPEF) and routed Design Exchange Format (DEF), rejects missing or
duplicate nets, and requires complete parasitic sections. Standard punctuation
escapes are decoded before comparing hierarchical names. Native signoff static
timing analysis (STA) separately requires zero unannotated and partially unan-
notated drivers and finite setup and hold slack meeting the fixed guardband.
The model cannot change clock period, uncertainty, output load, or verification
decks.

2.3   Repair and equivalence
The Anthropic response schema distinguishes scalar configuration actions from
exact RTL text patches. The safety checker limits writable fields according to
4       R. Vallabhan TV et al.

the deterministic failure class and selects the earliest affected stage. A physical
change invalidates downstream artifacts and evidence. Native rechecking must
cover the action-derived obligations before the change becomes a verified repair;
a model confidence value does not determine acceptance.
    EQY partition cuts can make related outputs independent even when they
feed joint next-state logic. The controller splits vector nets into scalar matches
and groups the matched signals to prove their joint relation. Counter and shift-
register controls pass this configuration; a control forcing a mapped counter-flop
input to zero produces a counterexample. A later successful strategy resolves an
earlier unknown only for the same partition, while a counterexample or unre-
solved terminal result blocks release. This is a correction to proof decomposition,
not a new equivalence algorithm.


3     Experimental Protocol

3.1   Designs and environment

The design set contains a register, registered multiplexer, eight-bit ALU, counter,
bidirectional shift register, four-entry FIFO, and a 1,024-stage serial shift regis-
ter. The latter supplies a larger synthetic case with 1024 mapped cells; it extends
scale rather than adding an independent application domain. Each design has
an authoritative self-checking testbench.
    The PDK is SKY130A at Volare revision bdc9412b3e46. We use the HD cell
library at its typical 25 ◦ C, 1.8 V corner (tt_025C_1v80). The native container
uses OpenROAD commit 41a51eaf4ca2 and KLayout 0.28.2; native pre-layout
OpenSTA reports version 3.1.0. Baseline constraints use 10.0 ns, 0.25 ns uncer-
tainty, and 0.05 pF output load. The stressed ALU uses 3 ns with the same
uncertainty and load. Physical parameters and complete commands are retained
in each run.
    The paired baseline flows use source fingerprint af31b39f6782; the nine live
repair episodes use the archived repair-cohort snapshot 18d948815ac6. Both
snapshots and exact commands are retained. The larger shift register uses two
placement-padding sites, 25% core utilization, 0.35 placement density, an 80%
conservative antenna prediction margin, eight native antenna repair iterations,
eight routing workers, and passive filler cells. These settings change implemen-
tation and repair effort; the final antenna gate retains the original rule limits.
Native antenna checking remains mandatory even when global-route repair pre-
dicts no violations.
    We run each selected design twice from RTL through signoff. Concurrent
jobs make elapsed time an observation of this environment, including container
overhead, rather than an isolated performance comparison. A separate extrac-
tor validates candidate hashes, gate contracts, lineage, and certification identity
before generating the tables. Cell area comes from the synthesis library report;
die area comes from routed DEF coordinates and database units.

3.2   Fault detection coverage

The six author-constructed fault classes in Table 3 are exercised three times
each. The unit of detection coverage is the fault class; the 18 executions are
consistency checks, not 18 independent trials. Stale-input trials run native DRC
on a physically different, clean ALU layout with the same top name, while the
candidate selects the current ALU layout. The rule-coverage trials disable front-
end-of-line (FEOL) checks in a copy of the real DRC deck. The LVS fixture writes
a database-shaped file and prints a match without calling compare. The layer-
map fixture performs a real stream-out with the mapping assignment omitted,
then runs native DRC. PDN fixtures remove via connections from a retained
DEF. The final fixture substitutes a foreign GDS after a verified ALU repair
while retaining the repair’s DRC/LVS records.
    The fixtures are chosen to exercise declared contract obligations. Coverage is
therefore a check of the implemented guards on these classes, not an estimate of
detection probability, superiority over another flow, or a sampling-based false-
pass rate. Same-top stale repetitions reuse the same two ALU candidates; other
fixtures span register, ALU and FIFO. Actual OpenLane checker executions are
reported separately with precise stage scope and unsupported obligations.


3.3   Live repair study

Three repeats are declared for each of three injected failures: unpadded ALU
spacing, an excessive register PDN strap offset of 50 µm, and ALU setup fail-
ure at 3 ns. Each episode uses Claude Sonnet 5 (Anthropic API; identifier
claude-sonnet-5), automatic typed authorization, and the recorded retry pol-
icy. Every declared episode remains in the denominator. Model proposals can
differ between repeats, and native tools determine the recheck outcomes. Both
recorded controller versions allow three attempts per stage and a global budget
of 24 diagnosis attempts. Authorized changes roll execution back to their ear-
liest affected stage and invalidate downstream evidence; rollbacks can therefore
produce more than three model calls in an episode. A previously failed stage fin-
gerprint with unchanged configuration and inputs stops the loop before another
identical attempt. In the larger follow-up, the two policy escalations use 7/24
and 3/24 diagnosis attempts, respectively; their retained failure reports identify
this no-progress stop rather than exhaustion of the global budget.
    A comparator retrospectively replays the same authorized proposals, label-
ing each as promoted without repeating native checks. Native outcomes are used
only to assess whether that promotion was supported. This replay fixes proposals
collected under native feedback; it measures support for these states, not the tra-
jectory or success rate of a separately executed controller that skips rechecking.
We evaluate this comparator at the proposal level, separately from the episode
outcome. A native failed recheck establishes a confirmed false-clean promotion;
an unavailable required recheck establishes an unsupported promotion. Earlier
development and provider-transport failures are tabulated separately in the sup-
plementary live-episode catalog.

4.2    Detection coverage
The controller blocks all six declared fault classes in all three executions per
class. Disabling FEOL produces zero markers but only 152 normalized categories.
Missing mapping also produces a native zero-marker report, while stream-out
checks detect incompatible layer shapes. The fake LVS database has zero com-
parison cross-references despite its match message. These examples demonstrate
the obligations exercised; author selection of the fixtures precludes an indepen-
dent comparative-performance inference. The follow-up executes actual Open-
Lane code from the same pinned tool image, including DRC, optional reference
XOR, Netgen report gates and native post-pdngen power-grid checks. Table 4
separates detected, not detected and not checked results within the executed
scope. Native power-grid checks detect the missing-via fixtures for register, ALU
and FIFO, with paired untouched DEF controls. Optional XOR detects stale,
foreign and wrongly mapped geometry when an explicit selected reference is
supplied. The stream-out interface requires a mapping option; this differs from
checking already generated GDS. The retained KLayout no-compare database
is incompatible with the Netgen report interface and is not scored as a semantic
detection.
    These are isolated stage and report-checker runs on externally generated ar-
tifacts, not a full OpenLane rerun. The unmodified DRC stage does not pass
this PDK deck’s runtime rule flags. Its known-violating control declares zero
categories and zero markers; the explicitly enabled native-engine control de-
clares 213 raw categories and four markers. These controls are identified sep-
arately. No detection-rate or full-flow superiority estimate is derived from this
constructed six-class matrix. Commands, stock checker source hashes, known-
fault controls, unsupported obligations and initial invalid control attempts are
retained. Selected-candidate binding, inventory approval and reference-free map
semantics are unsupported obligations in these executed interfaces. Their ab-
sence is not a measured false-pass rate.

changes. Table 6 preserves both arms. The original source repeats at +1.586

runs. Reapplying placement padding in later tool processes is part of the

candidate and gate identities continue to bind exact file bytes, so a changed

supplemental fingerprint matches in 6 of seven original pairs and matches in
the one-worker pair. Native release IDs remain distinct, because they also bind
raw GDS, reports and path-bearing characterization records. Timestamp-only
normalization of GDS therefore cannot stabilize the entire release identity by
itself.

skips. Skipped archived fixtures are not counted as passed verification. The sup-

cumulator’s spacing, antenna and diode-legalization failures and the ALU-bank
antenna failures. These failed runs are excluded from the clean-design count.


5   Related Work

OpenROAD and OpenLane supply the implementation infrastructure [1,2].
METRICS2.1 provides structured flow measurement and tuning [4]; PROV pro-
vides general provenance relationships [5]. ChatEDA studies LLM-based task
decomposition, script construction, and EDA execution [6]. ORFS-Agent stud-
ies iterative parameter optimization in OpenROAD-flow-scripts [7]. MCP4EDA
connects language-model interaction to a complete tool flow and uses backend
feedback for synthesis optimization [8]. FlowFixer-EDA describes log-grounded
diagnosis, patch validation, and native tool feedback for repair [9]. These works
12      R. Vallabhan TV et al.

establish that tool use and verified feedback already form part of LLM-assisted
EDA research. EDAid uses multiple model-controlled agents to improve EDA
workflow execution [10], extending the comparison beyond single-agent con-
trollers. Ghose et al. study executable OpenROAD changes and measured qual-
ity of results in Automated QoR improvement in OpenROAD with coding agents
[11].
    ChiBench supplies a curated RTL corpus for testing EDA tools [12]. DRVer-
ify addresses physical-verification runset testing with known legal and violating
layout structures [13]. Such work reinforces that a zero-marker count does not by
itself establish rule intent or coverage. EQY supplies the equivalence engine used
here [3]. The contribution of this study is the measured combination of native
false-clean fixtures, candidate-content lineage, and action-derived rechecking. We
make no unsupported priority claim that prior flows lack equivalent protections.
A comparative claim against another controller would require a shared design
set, tool environment, constraints, and verification obligations.


6    Discussion and Limitations
The fault fixtures exercise six selected evidence failures. They are constructed
coverage tests, and repetition does not add independent samples. The guard re-
jects them through several mechanisms: input identity for stale GDS, approved
inventory for missing rules, native comparison evidence for fake LVS, structural
connectivity for PDN, and layer mapping for stream-out. Hashes preserve iden-
tity under a trusted controller, host, tools, and PDK. They do not prove that
an approved rule deck implements every foundry requirement or that tool code
and gate records cannot be forged.
    The repair study contains only three fixture types and three repeats per type.
Its 7/9 outcome should not be generalized to unseen circuits or failure classes.
The larger shift register is a regular synthetic circuit; full camera, processor,
SRAM-macro, and multi-clock systems remain outside this evaluation. The de-
clared local signoff omits multi-corner timing, on-chip variation, electrical power
integrity, and foundry qualification. Formal equivalence establishes preservation
of the RTL relation, while simulation checks the supplied behavioral tests rather
than an external specification. The JSONL audit retains every accepted proposal,
native recheck metric, and provider call count. Rollback can reuse a numbered
attempt filename, so a complete raw prompt–response and intermediate-artifact
archive for every proposal is not established. Final native artifacts and the cohort
denominator are retained.


7    Conclusion
RTL2GDSAGI binds native verification evidence to a selected implementation
and requires native rechecking of authorized model proposals. The measured
flow completes 7 designs, including a 1024-cell synthetic shift register. All six
declared fault classes are blocked across their consistency checks. Of 9 live repair
                        Candidate-Bound Evidence for RTL-to-GDS Signoff         13

episodes, 7 complete signoff and 2 escalate; the stressed ALU result preserves the
3 ns constraint. Repeat checks separate same-source behavior from sensitivity to
controller changes. Separately, none of four larger accumulator repair episodes
certify: two stop under the controller’s no-progress policy and two are interrupted
by provider credit. The interruptions leave eventual repair outcomes unknown.
Repeated artifacts reproduce at the stated content and timestamp-normalized
levels. The evidence supports the tested release contract and identifies the re-
maining limits on larger applications and verification coverage.
